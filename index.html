    Space weather, translated

    What the sun is doing

    Kp index · global activity
    –

    Kp runs 0 to 9. Shetland can see a show from Kp 3. Central Scotland usually needs Kp 5 or more.

    Bz · magnetic field direction
    –

    When Bz points south (negative), solar wind energy pours into Earth’s field. A long negative Bz is when shows suddenly explode.

    Solar wind speed
    –

    Normal is about 400 km/s. Over 500 hits harder. Measured a million miles upstream, so it reaches us 30 to 60 minutes later.

    Why you keep missing it

    How to actually catch it

    Look north, low

    It’s on the horizon

    From Scotland the aurora usually sits low over the northern horizon, not overhead. Find a spot with nothing in the way to the north: a beach, a loch, a hill.

    Your phone sees more

    Point your camera

    To the eye a weak aurora looks like a grey-white glow or a cloud that won’t move. A 3 to 10 second night-mode photo shows the green. If the photo’s green, keep watching.

    20 minutes

    Let your eyes adjust

    Kill the headlights and stay off your bright screen. Full night vision takes about 20 minutes, and one glance at a phone resets it. Turn the brightness right down.

    Bursts, not all night

    Stay out, then stay longer

    Shows come in surges (substorms) that flare for 10 to 30 minutes, often between 10pm and 2am. Most people miss it by heading home 15 minutes too early.

    Clouds beat space

    Chase the gaps

    A Kp 7 storm is useless under thick cloud. If your area is socked in, the list above often has a clear spot an hour away.

    Dress for 2am

    Bring the flask

    Layers, a hat, gloves, something hot to drink, a head torch with a red light. Cold sends people home right before the show starts.
